      Methods

      How products were found, what counts as an AI echo product, and what each verification level means. Maintained for the ImageGuideEcho Registry.

      Inclusion

      1. The clearance appears on FDA’s Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Medical Devices list, or was found through openFDA under an AI-specific product code (QJU, QIH, QVD, QUO, SDJ, POK) with a cardiac ultrasound indication.
      2. The device input is ultrasound of the heart: transthoracic, point-of-care or handheld, fetal echocardiography, or procedural echo.
      3. Standalone software and ultrasound systems both qualify. A system clearance is included only when its FDA summary names a cardiac AI feature. System clearances whose AI feature is non-cardiac, or not identifiable in the summary, are listed under exclusions.

      Exclusion

      • ECG-based estimators of echocardiographic phenotypes (low ejection fraction, cardiac amyloidosis, structural heart disease). The input is an ECG, not an echo.
      • Digital stethoscope and phonocardiography software.
      • Cardiac CT and cardiac MR analysis software.
      • Ultrasound AI for other organs (bladder, lung, nerve, liver, breast, obstetric biometry).

      Sources and pipeline

      1. Inventory. FDA AI-enabled device list (CSV) and openFDA 510(k) endpoint queries by product code, applicant, and device name. openFDA is authoritative for decision dates, product codes, and applicant names.
      2. FDA summaries. The 510(k) summary or De Novo decision summary PDF for every clearance was downloaded from accessdata.fda.gov and text-extracted. Indications, predicates, changes, performance testing, dataset descriptions, and flags were read from those documents.
      3. Publications. PubMed and Crossref lookups for each product; a paper is shown as resolved only when its DOI or PMID resolved during extraction and its title matched.
      4. Verification. A second, adversarial pass re-read every quoted performance claim against the source text, re-checked dates and codes against openFDA, and re-resolved every DOI. Unsupported claims were removed.
      5. Build. scripts/build-data.mjs merges the verified research files with openFDA and writes data/products.js.

      Site quality metrics

      The Site Quality tab reports the nine quality metrics the registry lists as live, for every enrolled site, against a benchmark, plus four proposed AI-assisted reporting metrics kept separate from them.

      • Metrics. The nine metric names come from the registry. The values under them are generated by scripts/gen-registry.mjs from a fixed seed.
      • Benchmark. Two references are selectable: a registry target per metric, and the median across sites. Each metric carries a comparison direction, shown in the Comp column: ≥ when higher is better, ≤ when lower is better.
      • Colour. A cell is coloured by its percentage distance from the benchmark, in four steps each side plus a within-1% band. Blue is better than benchmark, red is worse, grey is within 1%.
      • N and D. Every metric has a numerator and a denominator. The denominator is the eligible population, which differs per metric: all studies for completeness, studies with a stenotic lesion for valve areas, eligible chemotherapy, heart failure or cardiomyopathy studies for strain.
      • Pooling. A quarter, a period total and a registry roll-up are all denominator-weighted, so they are pooled rates and not averages of rates.
      • Sites. Sites are labelled Site A to Site H. No real institution is named.

      AI-assisted reporting metrics (proposed)

      Four metrics measure how AI is used in the reporting workflow. They are proposed by this project, are not among the registry’s nine live metrics, and carry a Proposed mark wherever they appear.

      • AI-assisted report rate. Finalized reports carrying at least one AI-derived value with recorded algorithm provenance, over all finalized reports. It counts documented AI provenance in the signed report, not AI activity in the lab.
      • Accepted, edited, rejected. Three dispositions of a single AI candidate value: reached the signed report unchanged; kept but corrected beyond the field’s rounding tolerance; or discarded, replaced, or left out. They share one denominator and sum to 100%. Accept and reject alone would not, because the common interaction is correcting the AI result rather than taking or discarding it.
      • Denominator. The disposition metrics count candidate values, not studies: one study offers several automated fields. The AI-assisted report rate counts reports. The two are never summed or averaged together.
      • No better side. All four are scored against a reference with no direction. A lab that accepts every AI value has not improved, it has changed what its signature means; a lab with no AI cannot be scored at all. They are painted on a one-hue distance ramp, they carry no rank, and they are excluded from the below-benchmark tallies.
      • Not an AI error rate. A rejection records what one reader did with one number in a draft. No adjudicated truth exists on either side, and the AI value anchors the reader, so rejection understates true disagreement. Accuracy against a reference belongs on the AI Benchmarking tab.
      • Not submitted. A site that cannot export per-element AI provenance is shown as not submitted, never as zero, which would be indistinguishable from a lab that uses no AI.
      • Reference values. The reference for each is an illustrative placeholder. No measurement, guideline or published distribution sets it.

        Update procedure

        1. node scripts/refresh-openfda.mjs re-pulls openFDA records for every known submission and prints new AI-coded cardiac ultrasound clearances to review.
        2. Add new families to data/research/families.json, research them, and drop the verified JSON into data/research/.
        3. node scripts/build-data.mjs then node scripts/gen-registry.mjs.
        4. node scripts/validate.cjs renders the site headless, checks for console errors, and refreshes the screenshots.
